VISUAL TUTORIALS HELP MAPÚA STUDENTS LEARN SOFTWARE FASTER, STUDY FINDS

By Keane Zachary S. Dizon

Mapúa University students overwhelmingly favor visual tutorials over traditional text-heavy manuals when learning complex software, according to a recent study that examined instructional formats and their impact on software proficiency.

As digital tools become increasingly essential in higher education, students are expected to master applications such as AutoCAD, Fusion 360, and MATLAB while navigating the demands of Mapúa University's fast-paced trimestral system. However, a new study suggests that the format of learning materials may significantly influence how efficiently students acquire these technical skills.

The study, Analysis of Visual-Centric Tutorials and Software Proficiency Among Mapúa University Students, surveyed 43 undergraduate students who regularly use software applications as part of their academic work. The findings revealed a clear preference for visual-focused learning materials, particularly video tutorials and screen-recorded demonstrations, over traditional text-based manuals.

WHY STUDENTS PREFER VISUAL LEARNING

Many participants reported that visual tutorials made software functions easier to understand because they provided direct demonstrations of actions and procedures. Rather than interpreting written instructions and imagining how they should be applied, students were able to observe each step in real time.

One participant explained that visual tutorials "reduce mental load and make the steps clearer,” while another noted that seeing commands and interface elements on-screen makes software easier to navigate, especially for beginners.

Several respondents also reported difficulties with text-heavy manuals, citing challenges in visualizing instructions, identifying software tools, and processing lengthy written explanations.

to the study, these findings suggest that visual materials provide a more accessible learning experience by allowing students to immediately connect instructions with actions.

LOWER COGNITIVE STRAIN AND GREATER EFFICIENCY

A major focus of the research was the relationship between instructional format and cognitive load, or the mental effort required to complete a learning task.

Students rated the mental effort involved in using text-heavy manuals at an average of 3.91 out of 5, while visual tutorials received a significantly lower average rating of 2.88 out of 5.

The results indicate that students expend less mental energy when learning through visual demonstrations than when relying exclusively on written instructions.

Survey responses further showed strong agreement with statements that visual tutorials:
  • Improve understanding of software functions
  • Help users complete tasks faster
  • Increase confidence when using software
  • Enhance overall learning effectiveness

    • The results support established educational theories, including Cognitive Load Theory and the Cognitive Theory of Multimedia Learning, which suggest that learners process information more efficiently when visual and verbal elements complement one another.

    TRANSLATING INSTRUCTIONS INTO ACTION

    One of the study's most significant findings concerns the challenge students face when translating text into software actions.

    Many respondents agreed that written manuals often require them to mentally convert instructions into visual procedures before they can perform a task. This additional step can slow learning and create unnecessary confusion.

    In contrast, visual tutorials eliminate much of this interpretation process by showing users exactly where to click, what commands to use, and what outcomes to expect.

    Students described visual tutorials as providing immediate feedback and reducing the likelihood of mistakes. Several respondents noted that they frequently compare their work with tutorial demonstrations to ensure accuracy and build confidence.

    TROUBLESHOOTING BEYOND THE CLASSROOM

    The study also investigated how students respond when instructional materials fail to answer their questions.

    When faced with software-related difficulties, students most commonly reported:

    1. Searching for YouTube tutorials
    2. Using multiple learning resources simultaneously
    3. Engaging in trial-and-error experimentation
    4. Seeking assistance from peers
    5. Consulting external documentation

    These behaviors suggest that students actively seek alternative learning materials when official resources lack clarity or visual guidance.

    Researchers found that video tutorials were often the first resource students consulted when they encountered problems not adequately explained in manuals.

    RECOMMENDATIONS FOR EDUCATORS

    Based on the findings, the study encourages educators to adopt a more balanced instructional approach that combines visual demonstrations with concise written explanations.

    Rather than eliminating text-based resources entirely, many students recommended integrating screenshots, annotations, video walkthroughs, and step-by-step demonstrations into software instruction.

    Participants also emphasized the importance of keeping instructional materials updated and accessible while providing opportunities for hands-on practice.

    The research additionally highlighted the need for inclusive learning resources, recommending subtitles, transcripts, and other accessibility features for students with diverse learning needs.

    LOOKING FORWARD

    Although the study relied on self-reported perceptions from a relatively small sample of 43 students, its findings highlight the growing importance of instructional design in digital learning environments.

    As universities continue to incorporate advanced software into their curricula, the research suggests that how information is presented may be just as important as the information itself.

    For Mapúa University students balancing demanding schedules and technically challenging coursework, visual-centric tutorials appear to offer a more efficient path toward software mastery. By reducing cognitive strain and improving confidence, these learning resources may help students focus less on deciphering instructions and more on developing the technical skills they need to succeed.
